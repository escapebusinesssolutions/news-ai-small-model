# Stage 6 — Optimize

## Objective
Increase the economic value of TechSignal before adding substantial volume, new categories, or unnecessary infrastructure.

**Rule: optimize the commercial system, not the article count.**

Stage 6 begins only when Stage 5 produces enough external evidence to identify what is working. Until then, the system should remain in measurement mode rather than optimize against noise.

## Primary questions
1. Which topics attract qualified visitors?
2. Which articles generate affiliate clicks?
3. Which products receive the strongest commercial interest?
4. Which traffic sources produce the best click quality?
5. Which content earns revenue rather than merely traffic?
6. Where does the funnel lose users?

## Optimization hierarchy
Optimize in this order:

1. **Demand** — prioritize topics with demonstrated buyer intent.
2. **Content usefulness** — improve the decision support provided to the reader.
3. **Traffic quality** — favor sources and queries that bring commercially relevant visitors.
4. **Affiliate CTR** — improve product relevance, placement, and clarity without using misleading calls to action.
5. **Conversion economics** — identify products/categories producing actual orders and earnings.
6. **Production efficiency** — reduce cost and unnecessary processing only after the above are understood.

Do not optimize a downstream metric while ignoring a broken upstream stage.

## Funnel diagnosis
| Observation | Likely action |
|---|---|
| Low impressions | Improve topic selection, indexing, distribution, and search intent alignment. |
| Impressions but low clicks | Improve title/query alignment and usefulness of the page. |
| Traffic but low affiliate CTR | Improve commercial relevance, product presentation, and decision clarity. |
| High affiliate CTR but no orders | Investigate product fit, marketplace, price sensitivity, and sample size before changing the system. |
| Orders but weak earnings | Prioritize better-value products or higher-performing commercial clusters where evidence supports it. |
| Strong earnings from one cluster | Deepen that cluster before broadening the catalogue. |
| Strong traffic but weak commercial results | Do not mistake audience growth for business success. |
| Performance falls as volume rises | Reduce production and identify the quality or demand bottleneck. |

## Content optimization rules
- Improve existing useful pages before creating large numbers of replacements.
- Preserve factual grounding and catalogue controls.
- Do not invent product experience, testing, reviews, prices, specifications, compatibility, rankings, or awards.
- Do not create near-duplicate pages solely to capture adjacent keywords.
- Keep each article tied to a distinct buyer decision or problem.
- Strengthen internal links when they genuinely help the reader continue a decision process.
- Make commercial intent clear without turning the page into an advertisement.

## Commercial optimization
Use Amazon reporting as the commercial authority for orders, dispatched items, earnings, and related performance metrics.

Track, where available:
- Visitors
- Search impressions
- Search clicks
- Referral source
- Affiliate clicks
- Affiliate CTR
- Clicks per article
- Clicks per 1,000 visitors
- Items ordered
- Items dispatched
- Conversion rate
- Earnings
- Revenue per article
- Revenue per 1,000 visitors

Do not create duplicate measurement systems that contradict the authoritative source.

## Experimentation
Run small, attributable changes rather than simultaneous rewrites across the whole site.

Suitable experiments include:
- Different article structures for the same buyer intent.
- Better product-selection explanations.
- More useful comparison framing where catalogue evidence supports it.
- Internal-link placement.
- Clearer affiliate-link placement.
- Topic prioritization based on observed demand.

Avoid experiments that require pretending to have product experience or creating unsupported claims.

## Winner handling
When a winning article, product, or cluster is identified:
1. Verify the signal is large enough to be meaningful.
2. Confirm it is commercially relevant, not just high-traffic.
3. Identify adjacent buyer questions.
4. Improve the existing winner first.
5. Add a bounded number of genuinely distinct supporting articles.
6. Measure whether the cluster's total economics improve.

## Stop conditions
Stop or reverse an optimization when:
- The change reduces useful content quality.
- Affiliate clicks rise but reader usefulness falls.
- Traffic rises without commercial value and consumes production capacity.
- A result is based on too little data to distinguish signal from noise.
- The change introduces unsupported claims or weakens validation controls.

## Stage 6 success definition
TechSignal earns more from the same or better-quality audience because the system has learned:

**which people → want which decisions → about which products → from which content → through which channels.**

Only after that knowledge is repeatable should additional scale or expansion be activated.
